# Privacy boundary

This case study describes a contracted publishing programme at an aggregate level. It does not publish the network it operated.

## Published

- The programme goal and high-level architecture.
- Aggregate volume, distinctness, and reporting metrics.
- The categories of publishing connector used.
- The audit findings that changed how the programme was measured.
- The distinction between distribution volume and evidence of impact.

## Not published

- Platform account names, profile URLs, post URLs, client names, or source-video links.
- Article text, topic lists, transcripts, brand language, or publishing schedules.
- Credentials, tokens, database IDs, analytics properties, or webhook URLs.
- Screenshots, raw exports, crawler payloads, or platform-specific implementation code.
- Claims that the programme generated backlinks, human traffic, or AI citations when the available measurement could not prove them.

## Why this boundary exists

The strongest part of the work is the measurement audit. Publishing the network or its content would expose employer infrastructure and distract from the actual lesson: a system is not well measured merely because it produces a large number.
